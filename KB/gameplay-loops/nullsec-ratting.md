# Gameplay Loop: Null-Sec Ratting (2009–2014)

Ratting in null security space was the primary way most null-sec residents made ISK during this period. It was the backbone of the null-sec economy for individuals.

## The Basic Loop

### Daily Rhythm
1. Log in
2. Check local and intel channels
3. Undock in your ratting ship (usually a carrier or battleship)
4. Find an anomaly or belt
5. Clear sites for 1–3 hours
6. Dock up, store loot, repeat or switch to PvP / other activities

### Ship Choices by Era

**2009–2010 (Dominion era)**
- Dominix was extremely common
- Myrmidon for cheaper ratting
- Carriers were already strong but not quite as dominant as later

**2011–2012 (Crucible era)**
- **Carrier ratting** became the meta for serious ratters (Thanatos, Chimera, Nidhoggur, Archon)
- **Rattlesnake** started becoming popular
- **Ishtar** for more mobile/smaller scale ratting

**2013–2014**
- Supercarrier ratting became more common for wealthy players
- Marauders started seeing more use after certain buffs

## How Ratting Actually Worked

### Anomalies vs Belts
- **Anomalies** (Havens, Sanctums, etc.) were the main content after they were introduced.
- Belt ratting still existed, especially for lower-skilled characters or when farming specific rats (e.g. for tags or officer spawns). See [Asteroid Belt Rats](asteroid-belt-rats.md) for detailed spawn mechanics, behavior, aggro, special spawns, music cues, and era notes.
- "Ratting in a Haven" was the standard phrase.

### The Actual Gameplay
- Warp to site
- Deploy drones / launch fighters
- Manage your tank against incoming damage
- Watch local and intel constantly
- Move to the next site when finished

The gameplay was relatively low-attention after the first few minutes of a site, which is why many people ratted while doing other things (watching TV, chatting, etc.).

### Important Mechanics of the Time

- **Drone assist** was extremely powerful. Many people would have friends or alts assist drones.
- **Fighters** on carriers had specific behaviors and control schemes.
- **Bounties** were paid directly (no bounty system changes like later years).
- **Loot** from rats was often quite valuable, especially from certain factions.

## The Constant Threat

This is what made null-sec ratting feel different from high-sec:

- You were **always** at risk.
- Local could spike at any moment.
- Hotdrops were a real and constant fear.
- Many ratters kept a "panic" ship or cyno alt ready.
- "Ratting in a carrier while watching local" was a meme for a reason.

A successful ratting session often ended not because you got bored, but because intel said hostiles were coming.

## Economics of the Era

A good carrier ratter in 2011–2012 could make:
- 80–150+ million ISK per hour (depending on space and skills)
- This was considered very good money at the time.

This income was balanced by:
- The cost of fuel for the carrier
- The risk of losing a very expensive ship
- The need to live in space controlled by your alliance

## Social and Cultural Aspects

- Ratting was often done in "ratting fleets" where people shared sites and intel.
- Many corporations had "ratting taxes" or expected members to contribute through various means.
- Some people ratted on alts while their main was logged in doing other things (especially after multi-boxing became more common).
- "Ratter" was sometimes used as a somewhat derogatory term for people who only cared about making ISK and not participating in PvP.

## Era-Specific Flavor

- In the early part of this period, people still did a lot of belt ratting.
- POS fuel was a constant cost that many ratters helped pay for.
- "Blue donut" politics meant that in some regions you could rat relatively safely for long periods.
- The introduction and evolution of anomalies changed how people made money in space.

---

**Notes for Emulation**:
- Carrier and fighter mechanics should feel correct for the period
- Local and intel chat are critical parts of the experience
- The risk/reward balance should make ratting feel profitable but dangerous
- Drone assist and older control schemes should be considered
- The economy should reflect that ratting was a major ISK faucet of the time
