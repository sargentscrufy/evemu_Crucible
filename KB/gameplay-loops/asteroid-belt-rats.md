# Gameplay Loop: Asteroid Belt Rats (2009–2014 Crucible Era)

Asteroid belt rats were the classic "ratting" content in EVE during this period. While anomalies became dominant in null-sec, belt ratting remained a staple for new players, low-sec residents, and those chasing rare spawns (faction rats, haulers, officers). In Caldari space this meant primarily Guristas rats.

## Overview

- **Location**: Standard asteroid belts in systems (not deadspace anomalies or missions).
- **Purpose**: Bounties, loot/salvage, tags, and the lottery of rare spawns.
- **Difficulty**: Scaled with system security. High-sec (0.5+) had small, low-value spawns. Low-sec and especially null-sec had bigger, more profitable rats.
- **Era Context**: Belt ratting was very common early in the period (2009-2011). It was accessible with cheap destroyers or battlecruisers and served as entry-level PvE and a way to hunt for faction/officer spawns.

## Spawn Mechanics

- Rats spawned when a player warped into the belt or was present in it.
- Faction was determined by the region (Guristas in Caldari space like Venal or Lonetrek low-sec).
- Normal spawns: Mix of frigates, cruisers, battlecruisers, and battleships. Numbers and quality increased in lower true-sec systems.
- Respawn: Belts would repopulate after clearing or over time. Killing rats cleared the belt for new spawns.
- Special / Rare Spawns (the "lottery"):
  - **Faction Rats** (e.g. Dread Guristas): Stronger tank/DPS, higher bounties, dropped faction tags, modules, and ammo. Chance increased by checking many belts or in deep null.
  - **Haulers** (industrial rats): Often escorted by hostile rats. Dropped minerals, modules, or trade goods. Valuable for reprocessing or direct sale.
  - **Officers / Commanders**: Extremely rare (one-in-a-million in good space). Dropped highly valuable officer modules. Mostly null-sec.
- More belts checked or lower sec = higher chance for rares. Some players "chained" by clearing to force respawns.

## Behavior and AI

Belt rats used relatively simple NPC AI compared to scripted mission rats.

- **Aggro**:
  - Often auto-aggro on warp-in or proximity, especially in low/null.
  - Some ignored players until locked, shot at, or mining started nearby.
  - Drones reliably pulled aggro — rats would prioritize shooting drones. Players often used passive drones or careful aggression settings.
- **Combat Style**:
  - Orbit at range and apply damage.
  - Faction-specific EWAR: Guristas frigs used webs/scrams; some cruisers/BS had jammers.
  - Damage types matched faction (Guristas: primarily Kinetic/Thermal).
- **Waves and Reinforcements**:
  - Could appear in groups/packs.
  - Killing certain rats (e.g. specific cruisers or highest-bounty targets) could trigger waves or additional spawns in some cases.
  - Rare reports of rats warping out and returning with reinforcements (more noted in older 2007-2010 posts, less common later).
- **Mining Interaction**:
  - Rats could spawn while players were mining.
  - "Scout" rats (e.g. Guristas Ventures in some reports) could signal or trigger larger fleets if engaged or left alone.
  - Miners often used combat drones, smartbombs, or alts for defense.
- **Other Behaviors**:
  - Some rats could warp out (rare in high-sec).
  - In null-sec, spawns were larger with more battleships and better rare-drop odds.
  - No strong "trigger" mechanics like many missions — more static groups or simple reinforcement.

## Music and Audio Cues

- EVE used a dynamic/layered music system (primarily by Jón Hallur Haraldsson).
- **Ambient space music** played in quiet belts.
- When combat began (player locked/fired or rats aggroed and started shooting), the music would transition/cross-fade into **combat tracks**.
- This was the same general combat music used across the game (missions, sites, belts) — not a unique "belt rat" sting.
- No dramatic one-second "boss incoming" cue exactly on aggro. It was a state-based switch to more intense combat music once fighting started.
- Players from the era noted the music change as a clear signal that combat was active.
- Sound effects (targeting beeps, weapon fire, rat death sounds) were distinct from the music layer.
- In missions you sometimes got specific "mission music" on warp-in before combat music kicked in.

## Caldari / Guristas Specifics

- **Common Rats**: Pithi (frigs — webs/scrams), Pithum (cruisers), Pithatis (battlecruisers), Pith (battleships — potential jams).
- **Damage**: Kinetic/Thermal focus.
- **Typical Behavior**: Standard belt aggro + drone priority. Faction rats (Dread Guristas) prized for tags and modules.
- **Space**: Venal and Caldari low-sec/null were classic Guristas belt ratting areas.

## Era Notes (2009–2014)

- Very common for new players (high-sec/low-sec) and side income in null.
- Early period (2009-2011): Heavy belt ratting alongside early anomalies. Destroyer fits (Catalyst, Thrasher) and battlecruisers were popular.
- Crucible era: Drone boats (Ishtar, Dominix) and carriers became strong for null ratting. Belt ratting still used for specific farming or when anomalies were camped.
- Rare spawns were a highlight — "checking belts for officers" was a real activity.
- Changes were gradual; major belt reductions and anomaly dominance came later.
- Fits emphasized speed (afterburners), damage application, and constant local/intel awareness.
- In high-sec (0.5-0.7): Smaller rats, but occasional larger "rampage" spawns noted by miners.

## Simulation Notes for EvEmu

- Belt spawns should be entry-triggered (warp-in) with faction-appropriate rats.
- Simple group/pack logic rather than complex scripted waves.
- Drone aggro priority and basic EWAR (webs, scrams, jams).
- Rare escalation chances for faction/hauler/officer spawns.
- Music system (client-side) should switch to combat tracks on combat start.
- Optional: rare "call for help" or warp-out behavior on specific rats.
- Mining interaction: rats can spawn near miners and target them or haulers.
- Caldari space should feel Guristas-flavored (Kin/Therm, web/scram frigs, occasional jams).
