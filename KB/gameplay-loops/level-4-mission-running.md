# Gameplay Loop: Level 4 Mission Running (2009–2014)

Level 4 missions were one of the most reliable and popular ways for high-sec players to make ISK during this era. They were the "daily driver" for many solo or small-group players.

## The Loop

### 1. Agent Interaction
- You would have one or more high-quality Level 4 agents in a system you liked.
- Most serious mission runners maintained standings with a few different corporations so they could switch agents when one went on cooldown or offered bad missions.
- Standing grinding (using Social skills and connections) was a real part of the gameplay.

### 2. Mission Selection
Common desirable missions in this period included:
- **Worlds Collide** (very popular)
- **The Blockade**
- **Damsel in Distress** (when it paid well)
- **Gone Berserk**
- **Vengeance** / **The Mordus Headhunters**

Players would often decline missions they didn't like (especially ones that sent them into low-sec or had bad payouts), accepting the standing hit.

### 3. Ship Choice (Era-Specific)
Popular ships for L4s during this time:
- **Raven / Raven Navy Issue** (the king for most of the period)
- **Dominix** (especially with drone assist)
- **Maelstrom** (after some balancing)
- **Nightmare** (expensive but very strong)
- **Machariel** (if you were rich)

Fitting philosophy was different:
- Heavy use of T2 guns + T1 rigs in many cases (T2 rigs were expensive)
- Strong emphasis on tank (especially passive shield tanks on Ravens)
- Drones were extremely important (especially with drone assist mechanics)

### 4. The Actual Run
A typical mission run looked like this:

1. Undock and warp to the agent system (or already be there)
2. Accept mission
3. Warp to mission location
4. Clear initial wave(s)
5. Deal with triggers carefully (many missions had specific triggers that would spawn extra waves)
6. Loot and salvage (very important for income)
7. Return to station, turn in mission
8. Repeat

**Salvage** was a huge part of the income. Many players would run missions in a battleship, then come back in a dedicated salvage ship (often a Noctis after it was released in 2011, or a destroyer before that).

### 5. Income and Pacing

In 2010–2012, a good L4 runner could make:
- 30–60 million ISK per hour with good skills and a solid ship
- Significantly more if they were efficient with salvaging and mission selection

This felt like "good money" at the time, especially before some of the later inflation and income sources.

## Cultural Aspects

- Many players had "mission running alts" parked in high-sec while their main lived in null or low.
- There was a real sense of progression as you trained into better mission ships.
- Certain missions became infamous (e.g. ones that could suddenly spawn extremely difficult rats).
- Mission running was often done while watching streams, listening to music, or chatting on voice with corp mates.

## Risks

- **Ganking**: Especially during Hulkageddon periods or when running expensive ships. Smartbombing battleships in certain systems were a known threat.
- **Mission griefing**: People would sometimes try to steal your wrecks or otherwise harass you.
- **Boring missions**: The psychological cost of declining too many missions or running the same ones repeatedly.

## How It Felt

Level 4 mission running in this era felt like a proper job within the game. It was repetitive but satisfying. The combination of:
- Shooting big targets
- Managing tank
- Making money from both bounties *and* salvage
- The slow but steady progression of your character

...gave it a very particular rhythm that many players still remember fondly.

---

**Notes for Emulation**:
- Agent quality and standing mechanics matter
- Mission rewards and LP values should reflect the era
- Salvage income needs to be meaningful
- Drone mechanics and assist should behave correctly for the time period
- Ganking risk should feel real but not constant in high-sec
