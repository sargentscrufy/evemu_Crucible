# Gameplay Loop: Station Trading (2009–2014)

Station trading was (and still is) one of the purest forms of player-vs-player economic warfare in EVE. During the 2009–2014 period, it was especially powerful and accessible.

## The Core Loop

1. **Scan the market** for items with good margins
2. **Place buy orders** slightly above the current highest buy
3. **Place sell orders** slightly below the current lowest sell
4. **Wait** (sometimes minutes, sometimes days)
5. **Update orders** as the market moves
6. **Repeat**

This was often done while doing other activities (mission running, ratting on an alt, watching TV, etc.).

## What Made It Special in This Era

### Lower Competition in Many Items
While Jita was always cutthroat, many regional hubs and less-traded items had very good margins. A dedicated trader could make serious money without competing directly against dozens of other professionals.

### Different Tools and Information
- No modern market tools that many players use today
- People relied on spreadsheets, in-game tools, and manually checking prices
- "Market PvP" was very much about who was more attentive and who had better spreadsheets

### Capital Requirements
You could start with relatively little ISK compared to other high-end activities, but the real money required significant starting capital. Many traders would slowly grow their "trading wallet" over months.

### The Psychology
Good station traders understood:
- How to read order history
- When to push a market and when to let it breathe
- How to manipulate perception (large buy orders could scare competition)
- When to cut losses on a bad investment

## Popular Strategies of the Time

- **Regional trading**: Buy low in one region, sell high in another (required hauling alts or friends)
- **Meta trading**: Following ship and module trends after patches or big fights
- **T2 component trading**: Very profitable but required understanding industry
- **Plex trading**: Always active, somewhat safer but lower margins

## Risks and Downsides

- **Market manipulation**: Other traders could (and would) crash prices on items you were heavily invested in
- **Opportunity cost**: Your ISK was tied up in orders
- **Boredom**: It could be very passive
- **Scams**: Contract scams and other tricks were common

## How It Felt

Station trading during this era had a very particular flavor. It felt like playing the stock market inside a spaceship game. The best traders weren't necessarily the richest players — they were the ones who were most patient and observant.

Many people who "hated" the market still ended up doing some light station trading because the returns were so good compared to the effort required once you understood the basics.

For some players, it became their main activity. They would log in, update 50–100 orders across multiple stations, and log off having made more ISK than most people made ratting or missioning that day.

---

**Notes for Emulation**:
- Market order mechanics (update costs, ranges, etc.) should match the era
- Regional price differences should exist and be exploitable
- The feeling of "setting and forgetting" orders while doing other things is important
- Competition should feel real but not impossible for a dedicated player
