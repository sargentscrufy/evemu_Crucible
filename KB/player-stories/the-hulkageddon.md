# Player Story: Hulkageddon

**Year:** 2010–2012 (multiple iterations)  
**Written from the perspective of a high-sec miner, circa 2011**

---

I still remember the first time I heard about Hulkageddon.

I was sitting in a belt in a Retriever, happily chewing through a rock, when someone in local said:

> "Hulkageddon is back. Watch your back."

I had no idea what they were talking about.

Two hours later, my corpmate's Hulk exploded in the next belt over. He managed to get his pod out, but the ship was gone. He was laughing in voice chat.

> "They smartbombed me. Two battleships just sitting there at the belt. I didn't even see them until it was too late."

That was my introduction to one of the most infamous player-driven events in EVE's history.

## What Hulkageddon Actually Was

It wasn't an official event. It was organized by players — mostly Goons and other griefers at first — who decided that the best way to have fun was to declare open season on mining barges, especially Hulks.

The rules were simple:
- Kill as many Hulks (and other mining ships) as possible
- Post killmails
- Have fun

Prizes were sometimes offered for the most expensive kills or most kills in a period.

## How It Felt to Play During It

For miners, it was terrifying and exciting in equal measure.

Suddenly:
- You couldn't just AFK mine in peace
- You had to actually pay attention to local
- People started flying with combat ships on standby
- Some systems became completely dead because everyone was too scared to undock their expensive Hulks

There was a real psychological game happening. Miners would try to figure out which systems the gankers were in. Gankers would hop around trying to find targets. Some people set up bait Hulks.

I remember one time our corp moved our entire mining operation to a quiet system we thought was safe. We mined there for almost a week before someone found us. When they did, they brought smartbombing battleships and cleaned out three Hulks in one pass.

The guy who lost his ship was actually laughing about it in corp chat afterward.

> "That was the most fun I've had losing a ship in months."

## Why It Worked

Hulkageddon captured something special about EVE during that era:

1. **It was player-driven** — CCP didn't create it. The community did.
2. **It created real stakes** — A Hulk was a serious investment. Losing one hurt.
3. **It changed behavior** — Entire regions would go quiet or adapt.
4. **It created stories** — Everyone who played during those periods has a Hulkageddon story.

It also highlighted how different the risk profile was back then. Today people talk about ganking like it's a solved problem or something that only happens to stupid people. Back then, it felt like a genuine force of nature that could appear in any high-sec system.

## The Aftermath

After a while, people adapted:
- More people started using tanked Hulks instead of max-yield fits
- Orca support became more common
- Some just stopped mining during the events
- Others leaned into it and started hunting the hunters

When Hulkageddon eventually died down (or CCP made changes that made it less profitable), there was almost a sense of nostalgia for it among some players. It had become part of the culture.

---

**For EvEmu**:
Events like Hulkageddon are extremely difficult to simulate with AI, but they represent the kind of *emergent danger* that made high-sec feel alive. Even just having the historical context helps when designing NPC behavior or random events that can create similar tension.
