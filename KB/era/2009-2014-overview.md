# EVE Online: 2009–2014 Era Overview

This period represents one of the most iconic and distinct chapters in EVE Online's history. It spans from the **Dominion** expansion through **Kronos**, covering the transition from the old EVE into something closer to the modern game — while still feeling very different from today's EVE.

## Timeline of Major Expansions

| Year | Expansion       | Key Themes                              | Notable Impact |
|------|------------------|-----------------------------------------|----------------|
| 2009 | Dominion        | Sovereignty overhaul, supercapitals    | Changed nullsec forever |
| 2010 | Tyrannis        | Planetary Interaction                   | New income source for many |
| 2010 | Incursion       | Live events, Sansha invasions           | First major "world event" style content |
| 2011 | Crucible        | UI overhaul, quality of life, balance   | Often considered the peak "feel" for many veterans |
| 2012 | Inferno         | Missile changes, crimewatch rework      | Big combat balance shifts |
| 2012 | Retribution     | Crimewatch 2.0, new ships               | Security status and ganking changes |
| 2013 | Odyssey         | Exploration, scanning, data/relic sites | Made exploration popular again |
| 2013 | Rubicon         | Interceptors, mobile structures         | Small ship and deployment changes |
| 2014 | Kronos          | Marauders, mission running              | Boosted battleship gameplay |

## The "Crucible Era" Feeling (2011–2012)

Many longtime players consider late 2011 through 2012 (centered around the Crucible expansion) as the high point of EVE's atmosphere.

Characteristics of this time:
- The client still had a somewhat dated but very functional interface
- Capital and supercapital gameplay was dominant in nullsec
- POSes were still the main structures in space
- Incursions were new and exciting
- Hulkageddon events were a real cultural phenomenon
- Large coalitions (Goons, TEST, CFC, etc.) were solidifying power
- The game felt dangerous and meaningful

## Daily Life in New Eden (2009–2014)

### High-Sec Life
- Mission running (especially Level 4s) was a primary income source for many
- Station trading and market PvP were very active
- Hulkageddon made mining feel risky and exciting
- Faction Warfare was gaining traction but still relatively small compared to later years

### Null-Sec Life
- Sovereignty was held through structures and timers
- Large fleet fights were common but logistics were painful
- Ratting in carriers and supercarriers was extremely common
- "Blues" (coalition standings) were extremely important
- Spying and infiltration were constant

### Low-Sec Life
- Faction Warfare was the main organized activity
- Small gang PvP was highly valued
- Ratting and missioning in low-sec was risky but rewarding

## Technology and Mechanics That Defined the Era

- **Old UI** (pre many of the modern QOL windows)
- **POS mechanics** — fuel, stront, tower anchoring
- **Drone assist** was very powerful
- **Bumping** was a common tactic
- **Smartbombs** on battleships were terrifying in certain situations
- **Logistics** (especially Guardians and Basilisks) were critical
- **T2 rigs** were expensive and meaningful choices
- **Insurance** payouts were significant

## Why This Era Matters for Emulation

When building a private server targeting this period, the goal isn't just to have the right ships and modules. The *feel* comes from:

- How dangerous the universe felt
- How long it took to do things
- The social and political weight of actions
- The slower pace of information and coordination
- The specific balance of risk vs reward

This knowledge base aims to capture those intangible elements alongside the mechanical details.
